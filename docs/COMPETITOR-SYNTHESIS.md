## 2. Side by side

| | Synthesis Tutor | Anantha's Socratic math story (plan + demo) |
|---|---|---|
| Core idea | A conversational AI tutor with manipulatives and games | Mascot-driven animated stories, where Pip asks and the child acts |
| Wrong answer | "Targets gaps". Mechanics not public | The answer is **played out as if true** (ghost stickers, flipped story). Never marked wrong. Then a smaller question tied to the misconception |
| Hints | Tailored guidance; ladder not documented | Fixed ladder: a smaller question, then a visual cue, then a worked example with different numbers. The answer never comes first |
| Metacognition | Pre-test, "show me what you know" | "How did you know?" with three tap choices after a right answer |
| Adaptivity | Micro-assessments, mastery gating, spaced review, interest hooks | Per-misconception branching inside a problem. Pacing across lessons not designed yet |
| Voice | Live TTS tutor voice and speech input (beta) | No voice yet (scripted text bubbles) |
| Content | Proprietary K–5, in-house | Open, from public-school sources. One demo problem so far |
| Parent view | Cloud parent dashboard and reports | Planned: one app for kid and parent, on the device |

## 3. Assessment

**Where Synthesis is stronger**
- A live, warm tutor voice with read-aloud, which matters for ages 5–7 and is praised again and again.
- Real adaptivity across lessons: mastery gating, spaced review, interest-based hooks.
- Breadth: a full K–5 math scope, Spanish, Android, a parent dashboard and school sales already shipping.
- A team, money and distribution (35K+ families, 4.6★).

**Where Anantha's app is distinct and defensible**
- **Wrong answers acted out as consequences** is a sharper, more visible pedagogy than "targets gaps", and Synthesis doesn't show anything like it publicly.
- **Storytelling is the gap Synthesis's own users report losing** in the redesign. A story-first app goes straight at that complaint.
- **Privacy by design**: no account, no cloud, works offline. That is a real difference for privacy-minded parents, low-connectivity homes and schools wary of COPPA/FERPA vendor reviews. Synthesis collects device IDs, IP and geolocation.
- **Free and open source with open content** answers the "ran out of content / too expensive" complaints, because teachers can add problems.
- **Multi-subject from the start** (English and science), where Synthesis Tutor is math only.

**Honest risks**
- Synthesis is funded and shipping, with a live voice tutor and a head start on adaptivity, so the head-to-head on math alone is uphill.
- On-device only means no LLM tutor in the loop. Scripted Pip lines could feel static, which is the same "dry program" complaint Synthesis gets now.
- Animated stories take a lot of authoring per problem. The content-ceiling complaint will hit an open project even harder unless the generator (M2) works.
- Without voice, kids aged 5–6 who can't read fluently are mostly shut out.
- Open source with no backend has no clear way to fund itself or reach families. **[Inferred]** Schools and districts are the natural channel, but that needs standards alignment.

## 4. Takeaways for the plan
1. **Lead with "wrong answers become the story."** Make it the headline feature and test it against the "From Mistakes to Math Mastery" pitch. Keep the rule that nothing is ever marked wrong.
2. **Add on-device read-aloud early** (system TTS through Web Speech / AVSpeechSynthesizer, no network). Synthesis shows that voice is table stakes for ages 5–7. Speech input can wait, because their beta mis-hears answers.
3. **Design adaptivity across lessons into the event log now**: mastery gating, spaced review ("it's been 3 weeks"), all computed on the device from the local log. This takes Synthesis's best feature without a backend.
4. **Plan content scale in from the start.** Make the problem generator and an open, teacher-editable lesson format a core feature, aimed at Synthesis's most common complaint: "finished it in weeks".
5. **Make rewards about progress, not loot.** Have chests unlock new stories or mascots (as Synthesis unlocks games) and show streaks gently. Write a no-dark-patterns rule into the reward design, since parents praise Synthesis for avoiding "endless badges".
